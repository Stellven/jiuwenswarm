## How the recorder changed the next build

1. **DO, then LAB.** The first builder added an event recorder. On a 512-token prompt, it counted 52.69 GB of submitted expert copies during prefill and none during decode. Replaying the same traffic from warm memory took about one second. The serialized warm replay was 1.84% of the 54.37 s TTFT on that recorded request. A separate warm stock A batch had a 9.68 s median TTFT; it was a different run. [Recorder report and warm replay](OPS/iterations/spark-c1-functional/report-v3.md)
2. **REPORT, then a new plan.** The analysis combined routed-expert frequencies with request-level disk reads and page faults. The planner selected frequently used experts for the existing native GPU cache, leaving its normal replacement policy intact. JiuwenSwarm produced the change at `ad2130b0`, then the lab compared it with stock at the same cache budget. [Planner evidence and decisions](OPS/p3/cache-loop-native-decisions.json) · [Factory DO and lab](OPS/iterations/spark-c3-native-cache/pilot/iteration.json)
3. **Iteration 1 uncovered a negative result.** The first 30 GiB screen suggested faster prefill. Quiet ABBA reversed that conclusion. The seeded build took an additional 1.18 s and 2.84 s to return the first token, with similar decode. Most of the original decode jump from about 3 to about 20 tokens/s already came from stock’s native cache. [Quiet 30 GiB report](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-attribution/report.md)
4. **The analysis separated warm copies from cold storage reads.** At 60 GiB, sampled expert-page residency in host memory fell from about 65% in the CPU-expert reference to about 18% with the large GPU bank. Profile selection changed *which* host pages survived. In the matched 60 GiB run, GPU-seeded experts had fewer resident host pages and other experts had more. Lower prefill reads and fault counts support a page-cache explanation. [Post-request file-residency samples](OPS/p3/residency-iteration2-mincore-median-receipts.json) · [60 GiB mechanism report](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache60-build-overlap/report-v2.md)
5. **Iteration 2 tested direct reads into the seed buffer.** JiuwenSwarm revised the builder and produced `8e704490`. The lab measured the startup cost as well as request latency. Initial requests did less I/O, while none of the four warm timing intervals excluded zero. The follow-up planner first called for isolating direct versus buffered seed reads on one executable. After the 60 GiB and C4 reports, its next recorded decision prioritized a fully quiet 60 GiB confirmation with prediction off. The closing stock control has now completed under the amended quiet protocol. [Verified quiet report-v3](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache60-build-overlap/report-v3.md) [Builder decision and source](OPS/p3/cache-direct-seed-02b-source-product.json) · [Direct-seed report](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-direct-seed/report-v2.md) · [Seven report reads and successor decision](OPS/p3/post-c4-native-successor.json)

## Same-budget result at 60 GiB

Across ten pairs per size, C3 returned the first token 27.08 seconds sooner at 512 tokens and 92.07 seconds sooner at 2048 tokens than same-budget stock A+ by the paired medians. Each arm ran two fresh launches with the same 60 GiB bank, serving flags, and source conditioning. Greedy output and reasoning hashes matched at both lengths. The closing A+ launch prospectively replaced the entire build-overlapped control. The orchestrator disclosed an idle-priority file pull during the replacement. Its contribution to storage latency was not measured separately. ## Quiet 30 GiB comparisons

**The direct-seed revision shortened the first cold request in both launches. Its warm results were close to same-budget stock A+.** For 512 and 2048 tokens respectively, the paired median first-token savings were −0.024 s (conditional 95% interval −1.348 to +2.423) and +0.199 s (−1.719 to +3.261). The paired decode differences were +0.744 and −0.201 tok/s, with both intervals including zero. Both arms used the same 30,682.35 MiB allocation, matched greedy output, and completed ten pairs per size. [Verified direct-seed report](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-direct-seed/report.md)

During startup, JiuwenSwarm revision `8e704490` uploaded 31.664 GB to 3,686 slots through direct reads, with zero failures or fallbacks on either pass. On the first 512-token request, engine reads fell from 48.31 to 30.34 GB and from 43.42 to 29.92 GB. Across the two launches, request TTFT fell by 15.83 and 12.06 s. Measured from capped launch start through receipt of that first token, A+ took 137.63 and 127.69 s, and C3-direct took 125.66 and 126.63 s, saving 11.97 and 1.06 s. These two startup comparisons include the additional time spent seeding. [Frozen launch set](OPS/p4/cache-abba-direct-30g.json) · [Native analysis](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-direct-seed/analysis.json)

A page-residency sample on the reverse pass found 38.36% of the seeded-class file pages resident just after direct startup and 38.47% after stock startup. This comparison covers 1,844 seeded-class page draws and 6,020 other expert-page draws before the first request. Direct reads demonstrably filled the GPU slots. The sample gives no evidence of lower overall file-cache occupancy for seeded experts at server readiness. The next factory planner read this report and selected a comparison that runs both seed paths from the same executable. That experiment would isolate the contribution of direct reads from profile selection. [Mechanism and per-request reads](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-direct-seed/report-v2.md)

**Quiet confirmation reversed the exploratory 30 GiB result.** With ten paired requests per size across four independent launches in A+/C3/C3/A+ order, C3 added a median 1.18 s at 512 (conditional 95% interval −1.53 to +6.84) and 2.84 s at 2048 (+1.08 to +8.45). Paired decode gains were +0.19 and +0.03 tokens/s, with both intervals crossing zero. Stock A+ and C3 used the same actual 30,682.35 MiB bank and produced matching greedy outputs. The 30 GiB seed revision does not earn a warm speedup claim. [Verified P4 report](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-attribution/report.md) · [Native export](OPS/iterations/spark-p4-cache30-attribution/export/INDEX.md)

The quiet launches also reproduced a cold-request advantage at both measured startup positions. For the first 512-token request, A+ took 63.44 and 55.66 s across the two passes, and C3 took 44.63 and 47.55 s. Engine reads fell from 47.02/47.89 GB to 27.45/28.47 GB. For the following 2048-token request, TTFT fell from 103.66/97.56 to 77.57/70.76 s. C3 needed 25.01 and 20.26 s longer to become ready. Adding server readiness and first-request TTFT still favors A+ by 6.20 and 12.15 s before accounting for the client setup interval. These are two independent launch pairs, each comparing identical request positions. This startup behavior and the negative steady result describe different points in the server's lifetime.

## Measurement conditions

The two engines use byte-equivalent weights in their respective formats. Each launch uses a 100 GiB memory cgroup, zero swap, eight CPU cores and an 8 GiB system-available-memory watchdog. CUDA driver allocations on GB10 can fall outside the cgroup. Strata uses its frozen read-only routing profile, six experts, eight threads, 256-token prefill chunks, a disabled drafter and at least 16 GiB reserved headroom.

For the 16-position experiment both packs undergo the same file-specific conditioning. All sampled expert, dense and Engram source regions must show zero resident pages before launch. The client uses fixed 512- and 2048-token prompts, 32 greedy output tokens, and disabled prompt-cache reuse. The first six requests expose the warming curve. Five measured pairs follow, with per-request fault and read-byte counters before and after the first token. Results across repeated requests from a single launch describe that launch. The quiet ABBA compares request positions across two fresh launches per arm. Its intervals are conditional on those launches. [Prompt manifest](jiuwenswarm/ops/spark/prompts/MANIFEST.json)
