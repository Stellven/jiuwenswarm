The harbor town wakes before the sun. Fishing boats leave in a line, their lamps swinging as the water lifts them. On the hill, a baker opens the shutters and sets out bread to cool. A teacher walks to the school with a stack of corrected notebooks under one arm. The market fills slowly: first the vegetable sellers, then the people who repair shoes and radios, then the children who run between the stalls. By noon the boats return, and the price of fish is argued over in three languages. In the afternoon the wind turns, the clouds gather over the cliffs, and everyone moves their tables under the arcade. When the rain stops, the streets smell of wet stone and the lamps come on one by one.
