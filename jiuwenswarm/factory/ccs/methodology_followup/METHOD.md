# Report to proposed next input

Use `followup` after a completed or failed experiment report. Read the report and original task contract, select one unresolved question, and cite the episode evidence. The output proposes an objective, ontology slice, hypothesis, expected evidence, bounded experiment and capsule candidate. It is not an admitted task or changed contract.

Treat basic goals such as “make decode faster” as a reason to inspect multiple possible causes, not permission to change everything. Prefer the earliest missing prerequisite: recoverable evidence, correctness, repeatability, then bottleneck observation, then one-variable treatment.

The JiuwenSwarm agent decides what to do under its current contract. If the next input changes a scored workload, correctness rule or acceptance policy, prepare a new revision for operator admission/freeze. Do not inherit grants or source permissions from a report. Do not automatically activate a discovered method or component.

Keep failed/null ideas and resource costs in subsequent cycles. A generated candidate needs applicability, source mapping, bounded effects, positive and negative replay cases, correctness and observer checks, rollback and separate promotion authority. Missing measurements may prioritize inspection; they do not predict performance benefit.

To test a second cycle: preserve the first report, publish the proposal separately, admit any necessary task revision explicitly, run the next bounded action, retain its report, and analyze both reports. Verify that new evidence changes the unresolved question while the original contract and failure history remain intact.

## Lab-guided improvement capability

Call lab_guided with one scoped immutable JSON bundle artifact/observation ID. Schema factory.lab-guidance-input/1 includes evidence_ids, identities (source_commit, image_id, executable_sha256, model_manifest_sha256, contract_sha256), effective settings, quality (complete, correctness, independent_aa, aa_relative_spread), remaining (successors, lab_attempts), source_mapping and trace. Unknown telemetry stays absent. The existing diagnosis, hypothesis and ablation calls remain the starting methods.

This first extension recognizes only expert-reader-timeline/1 with a scoped evidence_id, critical_path_validated, decode_ms, uncovered_join_wait_ms and added_overhead_upper_ms. Missing identity/correctness/A/A/timeline returns collect_more; noisy A/A above5% cannot propose a speed treatment. Exhausted allowance or no positive net wait bound returns stop. A supported proposal emits factory.ontology-delta/1 and factory.draft-child-contract/1 linked by content hash, with observations, source ownership, alternatives, one treatment, falsifier and acceptance. The optimistic benefit ceiling is not a predicted speedup. These input quality assertions must be substantiated by cited lab evidence; the method is not an independent verifier.

The Friday planner records its own decision and may improve another CC or request observation repair instead of taking the proposed scheduling change. Task admission, source mutation, submission and acceptance remain in existing interfaces. At most one successor is permitted by the demo assignment; this method cannot widen that allowance. No new graph runtime or ontology service is added.

A retained failure bundle may instead identify model_intake, material_application, engine_activation, build or capture with a scoped evidence_id, pinned source_commit and bounded reproducer. The proposal then targets the responsible lab/builder/engine CC and emits a VerificationBoundary delta. Benefit stays unknown; acceptance is repaired evidence, never a speedup.

The startup-request-pairs/1 trace can instead identify a repeatable first-request excess with startup_warmup=false and two independent processes. Each process has a distinct process_id and cites evidence_id and positive first_ttft_ms, warm_ttft_ms and launch_to_ready_ms; source_mapping owner llama_server_startup must match the source pin. First TTFT spread must be <=10% and exceed warm TTFT by >=5x, in addition to the normal correctness/A/A gates. The proposed single change enables existing startup warmup. This is a readiness-policy hypothesis, not evidence of expert wait: launch-to-ready and launch-to-answer costs, unchanged warm throughput and fresh held-out exact outputs must be retained. Moving initialization before readiness is not an engine speedup. Benefit and cost estimates remain unknown.
